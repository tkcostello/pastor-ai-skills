# Tradition presets

Every pastor skill writes from inside the church's tradition. The profile's `TRADITION` line picks one of the presets below. If the line is missing, infer it from `DENOMINATION`. When the profile's own notes or `VOICE` line disagree with a preset, the profile wins: these are starting points, and churches inside one tradition differ.

Presets describe what a church in that stream treats as normal. They don't settle doctrine for anyone. When a piece of writing depends on a point the preset and profile don't cover (how the church baptizes, who may preach, what happens at the Lord's Table), ask or leave a `[placeholder]` instead of guessing.

## pentecostal-charismatic

Covers: Foursquare, Assemblies of God, Church of God (Cleveland), Church of God in Christ, Pentecostal Holiness, Elim, Vineyard, and nondenominational charismatic churches.

- The gifts of the Spirit (1 Corinthians 12 and 14) are for today. Tongues, prophecy, words of knowledge and prayer for healing are part of church life, not a debate to tiptoe around.
- Baptism with the Holy Spirit is preached and prayed for as a real experience. Use the church's own terms for it, and don't add doctrinal details (for example, whether tongues is the required initial evidence) that the profile doesn't state. Positions differ between these movements.
- Services often include ministry time or an altar response: prayer at the front, laying on of hands, anointing with oil (James 5:14). Announcements, emails and sermon tools can assume it.
- Expect God to move and say so plainly. Don't promise outcomes, and never invent testimonies of healing or answered prayer. Real stories come from the pastor.
- Women preach and lead in many of these churches. Foursquare was founded by a woman, Aimee Semple McPherson. Don't assume the pastor is a man.
- Foursquare churches summarize their message as Jesus the Savior, Baptizer with the Holy Spirit, Healer, and Soon-Coming King. Assemblies of God churches use their Statement of Fundamental Truths.
- Familiar words: ministry time, altar, the presence of God, Spirit-empowered, filled with the Spirit, worship (meaning the extended sung part of the service).

## reformed

Covers: Presbyterian (PCA, OPC, EPC), Reformed (RCA, URC, CRC), Reformed Baptist, Acts 29 and similar networks, and many confessional nondenominational churches.

- The doctrines of grace and God's sovereignty in salvation are assumed, not argued for in every piece.
- Confessions carry weight: the Westminster Standards, the Three Forms of Unity, or the 1689 London Baptist Confession for Reformed Baptists. Use the profile to know which.
- God grows his people through ordinary means: preaching, the sacraments or ordinances, prayer and the life of the church. Favor that language over "experiences" or "encounters."
- Presbyterian and Reformed churches baptize the children of believers. Reformed Baptists baptize believers only. Check before writing anything about baptism.
- Invitations are usually a call to repent and believe, not an altar call or a raised hand.
- Many Reformed churches are cessationist or cautious about the miraculous gifts. Don't present tongues or prophecy as normal practice unless the profile says so.
- Many restrict the offices of pastor and elder to men. Follow the profile's details rather than assuming either way.
- Familiar words: elders, session or consistory, means of grace, covenant, Lord's Day, catechism.

## baptist

Covers: Southern Baptist (SBC), American Baptist, independent Baptist, Baptist General Conference / Converge, National Baptist, and other Baptist churches.

- Baptism is for believers, by immersion, after a profession of faith. The Lord's Supper and baptism are ordinances.
- Each local church governs itself. Members vote on major decisions, so emails and letters about budgets, buildings or staffing often point to a members' or business meeting.
- Priesthood of all believers, personal faith and religious liberty are core convictions.
- Many services end with an invitation or response time. Evangelism and missions run deep: SBC churches give through the Cooperative Program and the Lottie Moon (Christmas) and Annie Armstrong (Easter) offerings.
- SBC churches hold to the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, which reserves the office of pastor for men. Other Baptist bodies differ.
- Baptists differ on Calvinism. Don't assume a position.
- Familiar words: deacons, members, Sunday school or small groups, invitation, ordinances, revival (in some churches, a scheduled series of meetings).

## evangelical

The default when no tradition is set, and the right choice for nondenominational churches that don't lean one of the ways above.

- Stay on shared evangelical ground: the authority of Scripture, salvation by grace through faith in Christ, the call to follow Jesus and share him.
- Don't take sides on secondary debates: Calvinism and Arminianism, the miraculous gifts, women in church leadership, baptism mode and age, end-times timelines. If a piece needs a position, ask.

## Other traditions

Methodist, Wesleyan, Nazarene, Anglican, Lutheran, Evangelical Free, Christian and Missionary Alliance, Churches of Christ, Mennonite and others: set `TRADITION: other` and describe the church's practice in the profile's Notes. Skills follow those notes and ask when a point isn't covered.
